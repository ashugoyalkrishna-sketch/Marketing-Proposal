The challenge

Marketplace growth is not just about listing a product.

Five things quietly decide whether a catalogue sells or stalls. Most brands fix one at a time — and the others keep pulling the numbers back down.

01

Product visibility

If a product does not surface for the terms buyers actually search, everything downstream — ads, pricing, reviews — is working against gravity.

02

Listing quality

Titles, images, bullets and A+ modules decide whether attention becomes a purchase. Thin listings lose the sale after the click.

03

Ad performance

Campaigns without structure spend across everything. Without search-term review and bid control, budget leaks before it converts.

04

Inventory control

Stock split across warehouses and marketplaces goes out of sync fast. A stockout costs the sale, the rank and the momentum behind it.

05

Marketplace operations

Account health, cases, returns, removals and compliance take daily attention. Left unattended, they turn into listing suppressions.

That's where Marketing Hunk comes in.

End-to-end workflow

How the work actually runs.

Eight stages, run in sequence at onboarding and then as a continuous loop once the account is live.

01

Market & product research

Category structure, competing listings, price bands and the search terms buyers actually use.

02

Listing creation

Catalogue build, variation structure, attributes and compliant product data.

03

Content & A+ development

Image gallery, infographics and brand content written and designed for the detail page.

04

Marketplace SEO

Titles, bullets and backend search terms aligned to indexed, relevant keywords.

05

PPC & advertising

Campaign structure, targeting, bids and search-term review on a fixed cadence.

06

Inventory monitoring

Stock position across channels, low-stock flags and replenishment planning.

07

Performance analysis

Sales, traffic and SKU-level reporting read together rather than channel by channel.

08

Continuous optimization

What the data says goes back into content, bids and stock planning. Then it repeats.
